NNS time-series prediction interval benchmark: corrected protocol

The earlier numerical ranking on this page is withdrawn. Its NNS call included scored outcomes in the optimizer's internal validation data, while the other methods used observed one-step lags and NNS forecast a longer chunk from one origin. Those scores cannot support a comparative performance or coverage claim. The original material remains available in Git history at commit 1075a338d932db13a8210e8ed23fb94f70a3f21f.

Reproducible evaluation

The executable source is nns_arma_interval_benchmark.R. It retains the simulated nonlinear heteroskedastic process and the existing comparison methods, with these corrections:

  1. All methods issue one-step forecasts from the same chronological origins. MAX_H is one and any different value is rejected.
  2. NNS receives only the observations available at the forecast origin and an explicit h = 1. Seasonal candidates are selected from the earlier internal training prefix. The remaining observed prefix is internal validation; the next outcome is scored only after forecasting.
  3. Baseline estimation, calibration, and scored test periods remain separate. Online calibration methods may use a test outcome only for later forecasts.
  4. Native NNS interval endpoints are scored for coverage, width, and interval score. A lower endpoint, point forecast, and upper endpoint do not specify a predictive distribution. CRPS and logscore are therefore NA for NNS, as for other interval-only methods. A degree-two partial-moment transform of three supplied points would define an additional modelling choice, not a native predictive density or a validated probability calibration.
  5. Each run records the selected periods, method, weights, shift, forecast origin, and horizon, together with raw per-seed scores. Keep package versions, sessionInfo(), seeds, and optional dependency availability with results.
source("examples/nns_arma_interval_benchmark.R")
result <- run_all()
sessionInfo()

The full five-seed, 3,500-point experiment now entails thousands of honest one-step optimizer fits and can take substantial time. The corrected source is provided for that experiment; this page does not substitute a small smoke run for a full benchmark or carry forward the invalid historical numbers.

What can be concluded

The comparison estimates performance for this process, seed set, horizon, training policy, and loss. Report uncertainty across series or origins and inspect coverage by volatility regime. The true conditional oracle is a reference with privileged information, not an available forecasting method. Conformal finite-sample guarantees require the applicable exchangeability or other calibration assumptions; this serially dependent, changing process does not satisfy ordinary iid split-conformal assumptions automatically. NNS residual bands also require empirical out-of-sample validation.

The baseline uses glmnet ridge when installed and lm otherwise; these are separate configurations and must be identified in results. Model refitting policies are likewise part of the compared procedure: NNS updates its fit at origins while the original lagged baseline is fitted on the initial training window. A claim about estimator quality alone requires an additional matched refitting-policy comparison.
